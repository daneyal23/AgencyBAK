# GTM strategy

## Tagline

Who to sell to. What to say. What to ignore.

## The stop line

If your “strategy” is a deck that never becomes a list, a sequence, and a CRM stage, it is not strategy. It is homework. We stop when the plan is runnable: named accounts, named messages, named stages — and a clear list of what you will not chase.

## In plain English

GTM strategy here means deciding the commercial path before you spend on tools or ads. We define the market you can actually reach, the offer that earns a reply, and the motion that moves a stranger into a held conversation. The output is not a thesis. It is a working system: target list logic, outbound sequences, CRM stages, and rules for what gets ignored so the team stays focused.

When the offer is still unproven, we do not pretend a full machine is ready. We start with roughly fifty hand-written emails — enough to learn who responds and why — before you scale list size, automation, or paid spend.

- Owner-led companies in the US, Canada, UK, Ireland, EU, Australia, New Zealand, and the GCC that want clarity without opaque agency theater
- Teams tired of vanity ICP slides and ready to own the accounts, CRM, and messaging

## What you get

- A written ICP and anti-ICP: who you sell to, and who you stop pursuing
- Offer and message angles tied to real buying jobs, not brand slogans

## Benefits

- You avoid burning domains, ad budget, and morale on the wrong accounts

- You leave with assets you own, not a locked strategy binder

## Under the hood (technical)

- ICP criteria as filters: firmographics, trigger signals, exclusions, and tiering rules (e.g. 1–4) that map to list builds
- Message architecture: problem → proof → ask, with variants per persona and channel
- Sequence design: step timing, channel handoffs, stop conditions, and reply routing
- CRM stage model: definitions for each stage, required fields, and what “held meeting” means in your stack
- Handoff artifacts: list schema, sequence copy pack, stage definitions — ready for Clay/enrichment, HubSpot/Salesforce/Zoho, or WhatsApp CRM workflows
- Proof protocol: small manual send volume, logged outcomes, revise-or-scale decision before tooling spend

## How it works (finish → start)

1. **Finish** — You can open the CRM and see stages that match the strategy; the list criteria are written; sequences exist; the team knows what to ignore; and — if the offer was unproven — you have reply evidence from a small hand-email test.
2. **Lock** — Operating brief, stage definitions, and copy pack so outbound, web, and ads (if used) point at the same accounts.
3. **Translate** — ICP into list logic; offer into messages and sequence steps.
4. **Pressure-test** — Who you sell to, what you say, and what you refuse — against how buyers in your markets actually buy.
5. **Start** — Short discovery on offer, proof, current pipeline, and constraints. No 40-slide kickoff theater.
