## 1. Which agencies serve GCC SMEs with CRM, RevOps, lead gen, GTM or automation, and are active in 2025–2026? (partner tiers, vertical focus)

### Takeaway
The GCC field splits into five groups:
1. HubSpot agencies at the top partner tiers in Dubai and Riyadh (NEXA, OneMetric, SelasM, House of Comms). They lean mid-market and B2B.
2. Zoho and Odoo partners that sell CRM bundled with ERP and ZATCA/e-invoicing compliance to SMEs, especially in KSA.
3. Low-cost Bitrix24 Gold partners in Dubai.
4. Salesforce partners, mostly mid-market, though a few claim a small-business focus.
5. Dubai-based outbound and lead-gen shops. Several of these (Danish Lead Co, oneaway) mostly serve clients in Europe and the US rather than GCC SMEs.

I found no agency that clearly combines all four of the following for traditional GCC SMEs (F&B, trading, construction, clinics): CRM implementation, outbound, Clay/n8n GTM engineering, and strategy.

### Inferences
- **Full-service firms probably make up a large share of the GCC competition** for the expanded service set, given the client's added web, ads, SEO and WhatsApp-bot services. But this session could document only about three that also have a CRM practice (NEXA, House of Comms, MO Agency), and none with public bundle prices. Snapchat and TikTok ads are named in the client's scope. No source here addresses GCC agency pricing for those channels.
- **Partner tier is the main trust signal for CRM agencies in the GCC.** Agencies lead with "Elite", "Diamond", "first in the GCC" or "Premium" (NEXA, OneMetric, SelasM, Arabian Software). A new remote agency without a tier will need to substitute case studies or a partnership with a tiered partner.
- **Vertical focus is rarely stated.** Real estate is the only GCC vertical named explicitly (CRMotive, plus HubSpot's KSA real-estate filter pages). I found no GCC agency positioned on F&B, construction, logistics or trading/distribution CRM-plus-outbound. That is a possible gap, though the evidence is thin because the pages couldn't be fetched.
- **Two distinct buyer worlds.** (a) "CRM + ERP + e-invoicing" SMEs, served by Zoho, Odoo and Bitrix24 partners and mostly priced per licence or project. (b) B2B and tech firms, served by HubSpot top-tier partners and outbound/RevOps shops priced at $3k–6k+ per month. The client's ICP of traditional SMEs and food businesses sits mostly in world (a), where few agencies sell outbound or GTM engineering.
- **Dubai-based outbound shops often sell outside the region** (Danish Lead Co, oneaway). Dubai serves as a base for them, which suggests GCC-market outbound for local SMEs is less crowded than the agency count implies.
- **Foreign agencies with a Dubai landing page and offshore delivery** (MO Agency from South Africa, CRMotive from Egypt, DevCommX city pages) show that a remote, Pakistan-based delivery model is already accepted at some level.

## 2. How do they price and package? (AED/SAR/USD public price points, with dates)

### Takeaway
Few GCC agencies publish prices. Public USD anchors: managed outbound **$3k–6k/month** (3-month minimum is common), managed RevOps **from $6k/month**, offshore-style appointment setting at **$50–99/hour**, and HubSpot SME projects starting under **$2.5k**. CRM software is cheap (Zoho CRM about **AED 55–200 per user per month**), so most of the SME spend is on setup and integration, which local partners quote privately.

## 5. Constraints on outbound and data (UAE PDPL, KSA PDPL, telemarketing/SMS, WhatsApp policies, cold-email norms)

### Takeaway
**The UAE's telemarketing rules (Cabinet Resolutions 56 and 57 of 2024) cover calls, SMS and marketing messages on social apps such as WhatsApp.** They require 9am–6pm contact windows and respect for the Do Not Call Registry, carry fines reported up to AED 150k, and apply in free zones too. Both the UAE and KSA default to prior consent for consumer marketing.

The UAE PDPL's executive regulations were still not issued as of June 2026. KSA's PDPL is in force and enforced by SDAIA, with fines up to SAR 5m, and SDAIA opened a consultation on revising the direct-marketing consent rules in October 2026. No source sets out a clear rule on B2B cold email in either country.

### Cited Findings
**UAE — telemarketing (calls, SMS, social-media messages)**
- Cabinet Resolutions 56 and 57 of 2024 took effect **27 August 2024**. They apply to all companies operating in the UAE, **including free zones**, and cover calls from landlines and mobiles, **marketing SMS and marketing messages through social media applications** — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2024/07/uae-tightens-telemarketing-regulations-what-you-ne); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/fr/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2024/07/telemarketing-in-an-evolving-legal-landscape-uae-adopts-regulations-on-telemarketing-activities); official text: [UAE Legislation](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/2519/download)
- Contact hours are **9:00am–6:00pm**. The TDRA runs a Do Not Call Registry (DNCR). Calls must come from locally registered numbers, and callers must identify themselves and the purpose at the start — [LY Lawyers](https://lylawyers.com/telemarketing-regulations-in-the-uae); [Middle East Briefing](https://middleeastbriefing.com/news/?p=3124); [Maqsam](https://support.maqsam.com/new-telemarketing-regulations-in-the-uae)
- Fines, as reported: AED 50,000 for a first DNCR violation, AED 75,000 for a second, and AED 150,000 for a third and after, plus possible suspension or licence revocation — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/new-telemarketing-regulations-introduced-in-uae/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/advertising-marketing-branding/1495836/new-telemarketing-regulations-introduced-in-uae). Under the telecom law the range is AED 5,000–200,000, according to a different source — [LY Lawyers](https://lylawyers.com/?p=25562). The sources conflict, and the exact schedule was not checked against the official text.
- One secondary source says TDRA approval is required before telemarketing. This is **not confirmed** — [LY Lawyers](https://lylawyers.com/telemarketing-regulations-in-the-uae)

**UAE — data protection and consent**
- Consumer Protection Law default: companies may not use consumer personal data for promotion or marketing without prior consent — [Baker McKenzie](https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/global-data-and-cyber-handbook/emea/uae/topics/cookies-online-tracking-and-direct-marketing); [DataGuidance](https://www.DataGuidance.com/opinion/uae-overview-direct-marketing-requirements)
- PDPL (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) gives data subjects a right to object to and stop direct-marketing processing, and consent can be withdrawn at any time — [Mondaq 2022](https://www.mondaq.com/compliance/1229090/compliance-%7c%7c-communications-with-customers); [The Brand Berries](https://thebrandberries.com/?p=21922)
- **The PDPL Implementing Regulations "have still not been issued" (June 2026).** The UAE has created a new federal authority for AI and data, and how it will split duties with existing regulators is unclear — [Morgan Lewis, June 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/06/uae-establishes-federal-authority-for-artificial-intelligence-and-data). Another source agrees they are pending — [ConsentStack](https://consentstack.io/regulations/uae-pdpl); [UpperSetup, May 2026](https://uppersetup.com/en/article/uae-personal-data-protection-law-pdpl-the-complete-business). An outlier blog claims they were issued, with a compliance date of 1 January 2027; its account is internally inconsistent — [PromptQuorum](https://www.promptquorum.com/local-llms/uae-pdpl-data-sovereignty-local-ai-2026)
- The UAE Data Office is the PDPL regulator (established by FDL 44/2021) and can fine and restrict processing. No 2026 enforcement actions were found — [ConsentStack](https://consentstack.io/regulations/uae-pdpl)

**KSA — PDPL and spam rules**
- General rule: **prior consent of recipients** is needed for advertising and marketing communications, the sender's identity must be stated, and opt-out must be easy — [Al Tamimi](https://turtl.tamimi.com/story/law-update-issue-367-saudi-arabia-and-competition/page/11); [Latham & Watkins, Dec 2023](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2023/12/Saudi-Arabias-data-protection-law-enters-into-force)
- The CST's Regulations for Curbing Spam Messages & Calls run in parallel with the PDPL — [Al Tamimi](https://turtl.tamimi.com/story/law-update-issue-367-saudi-arabia-and-competition/page/11)
- Penalties: up to **SAR 5 million**, doubled for repeat violations. SDAIA enforces — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2023/12/Saudi-Arabias-data-protection-law-enters-into-force)
- **9 October 2026:** SDAIA opened a public consultation on amendments to the PDPL Implementing Regulation that would revise the consent framework for direct marketing and strengthen withdrawal and opt-out — [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/news/sdaia-opens-public-consultation-on-proposed-amendments-to-the-pdpl-implementing-regulation/). An earlier 2025 consultation proposed removing the sender-identity obligation — [Clyde & Co, May 2025](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/05/saudi-arabia-new-pdp-law-consultation). Another summary says the consent requirement remains — [Wattlecorp](https://www.wattlecorp.com/recent-amendments-to-saudi-arabias-pdpl/)
- A vendor guide covers PDPL consent collection for business SMS in KSA — [Unifonic](https://www.unifonic.com/en/resources/how-and-when-to-collect-consent-a-guide-to-pdpl-compliant-practices-for-business-sms-messaging)

**WhatsApp Business Platform (2026 changes)**
- From **1 October 2026**:
  - marketing rates rise in KSA, the UAE, Kuwait and "Other Middle East"
  - **service messages are charged after the first 1,000 free per business number per month**
  - utility messages inside the 24-hour window are **no longer free**
  - Source: [YCloud](https://www.ycloud.com/blog/whatsapp-api-message-pricing-update-effective-october-1-2026); official reference: [Meta pricing docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)

### Inferences
- **Cold WhatsApp or SMS outreach to UAE consumers is effectively regulated telemarketing.** It needs DNCR checks, the 9–6 window, sender identification and, by default, prior consent. For consumer-facing SME clients (restaurants, clinics), WhatsApp should be used for opted-in leads and existing customers, not for cold prospecting.
- **B2B cold email** to business addresses isn't explicitly addressed in the sources found. The safe norm: role-based business contacts, clear sender identity, a one-click opt-out, honouring objections quickly, and minimal personal-data enrichment. In KSA, "prior consent" language plus the October 2026 consultation make cold outreach riskier than in the UAE. Legal review is advisable before running KSA campaigns.
- **Enrichment and data handling** (Clay or Apollo-style waterfalls): under both PDPLs, storing GCC personal data in tools hosted outside the region raises cross-border transfer questions, which are unresolved in the UAE because the executive regulations are missing. The agency should keep a data-processing agreement and opt-out logs per client.
- The October 2026 WhatsApp pricing changes raise the running cost of the WhatsApp automations an agency sells. Packages should pass through Meta fees or cap them.

### Gaps
- Whether **B2B** telemarketing, meaning calls to business numbers, is exempt from the UAE resolutions was not established.
- The **DIFC and ADGM** data-protection regimes, and the laws in **Qatar (PDPPL), Bahrain (PDPL), Oman (PDPL) and Kuwait**, were not researched (budget exhausted).
- Meta's **WhatsApp marketing frequency caps** and its 2026 policy on AI chatbots in WhatsApp were not verified.
- The full text of the October 2026 SDAIA consultation was not read (fetch blocked).

2. **"Compliance-adjacent" growth bundles:** while a partner handles ZATCA or UAE ASP invoicing, the agency sets up the CRM pipeline, quote-to-invoice sync and automation (n8n), sold to SMEs at their deadline.
3. **GTM engineering for non-SaaS SMEs:** Clay, n8n and AI enrichment for B2B services and traditional SMEs (contractors, logistics, wholesale). GCC Clay specialists such as oneaway target SaaS.
4. **Fixed-price, transparent packages.** Most GCC agencies don't publish prices (see section 2), and price transparency itself could differentiate. The agency's planned web app (portal, live pipeline dashboard) could support this.

**Risks and mitigations**
- **Trust and local presence:** competitors open offices and advertise "physical office in the GCC". Mitigations: a local WhatsApp and phone number, a UAE free-zone licence or a local reseller partner if the pipeline justifies it, GCC case studies, and Arabic-capable collateral for KSA.
- **Partner tier:** without a HubSpot or Zoho tier, sell with or through tiered partners, or start in Zoho or Odoo, where SME demand is concentrated.
- **Compliance exposure:** cold WhatsApp or SMS for clients in the UAE falls under the telemarketing resolutions. KSA marketing needs prior consent and SDAIA rules are changing. Build DNCR and consent checks plus opt-out logging into the agency's own app and offers.
- **Time zones and working week (general knowledge, not verified this session):** Pakistan (UTC+5) is one hour ahead of the UAE (UTC+4) and two ahead of KSA (UTC+3), so overlap is very good. The UAE private sector mostly works Monday–Friday, while KSA works Sunday–Thursday. Schedule KSA meetings accordingly.
