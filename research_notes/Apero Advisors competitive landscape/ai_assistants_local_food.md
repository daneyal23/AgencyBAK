## 1. Which restaurant AI phone-ordering and AI-host vendors are active in 2026, what do they cost, and what results do they claim?

### Takeaway
At least eight restaurant vendors are clearly active in 2026: Loman, Slang.ai, Kea, Maple, Popmenu, Hostie, ConverseNow and Voicify, plus challengers such as Revmo, Certus and Cardamom. Published SMB prices run about $150–$600 per location per month, flat with unlimited minutes. The vendors split into two groups. Order-takers that push tickets into the POS (Loman, Kea, Maple Pro, ConverseNow, Voicify) are aimed at pizza, takeout and QSR. Reservation and inquiry "AI hosts" (Slang, Hostie) are aimed at full-service restaurants. Every results figure is vendor-reported, and the only industry-level data shows low adoption: 6% of operators use AI to take orders.

### Inferences
- For an independent café, pizza shop or food truck, the realistic software cost in 2026 is $150–$400/month per location (Maple Pro, Loman, Slang entry tiers), and annual billing roughly halves it at Maple. An agency setup or retainer would sit on top of that.
- The ordering-versus-reservations split should drive vendor choice. Takeout-heavy operators (pizza, wings, Asian takeout, food trucks taking phone pre-orders) need in-call ordering into the POS. Full-service restaurants need reservation sync (OpenTable, SevenRooms, Resy).
- Results claims cluster around three metrics: share of calls automated (50–60%), answer rate (97–100%) and revenue or labor uplift (+20–26% revenue, −12–17% labor). None is independently audited, so an agency should run its own baseline-versus-after measurement as a selling point.
- The market is crowded and prices are falling: Popmenu pulled its public price, and Maple undercuts with annual billing. Software vendors are competing on price, which leaves implementation and integration as the space for agencies.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) study of restaurant AI phone ordering accuracy or uplift was found.
- No current pricing for Kea, Hostie, Voicify or ConverseNow (enterprise), and no 2026 ConverseNow news.
- Restaurant Business and QSR Magazine 2026 coverage did not surface in searches.
- No restaurant-specific AI phone vendors were confirmed for UK/IE, EU, AU/NZ or the GCC. Gulf restaurant ordering appears to run on WhatsApp instead (see Q3).
- No data was found on SoundHound, Toast or Square native AI phone products in 2026; they were not searched.

## 2. Which AI receptionist vendors serve local services businesses (home services, clinics, real estate), and at what price?

### Takeaway
Horizontal AI receptionists for US SMBs cost about $49–$299/month, metered by minutes or unique callers. Vertical tools such as Avoca (trades) and EU clinic tools such as Aaron.ai/Doctolib (from €119) price higher or by quote. UK prices cluster at £20–£300/month, with done-for-you options at £200–350/month plus a £500–750 setup fee. Australian prices run about A$50–700/month. Gulf buyers are pushed toward Arabic-first vendors with in-country data hosting. Overage rules and telephony fees are the main hidden costs.

## 3. Which agencies build or resell AI voice/chat/WhatsApp agents for small businesses (including Gulf WhatsApp-automation agencies), and how do they price setup and monthly fees?

### Takeaway
The common agency model is a one-time setup fee plus a monthly retainer with bundled minutes. Typical prices are $300–$1,000 setup (up to $3,000–5,000 with CRM integration) and $149–$1,000/month, with overage resold at $0.25–0.50/min against a cost of about $0.09/min. Most small agencies resell HighLevel's AI Employee or white-label platforms (Frontdesk at $54.99 wholesale, Answrr at $0.12/min). In the Gulf, WhatsApp agencies charge from AED 199/month for managed WhatsApp, about AED 5,000 to set up an AI agent, and AED 55,000+ for custom builds, plus Meta per-message fees.

## 4. What do owners complain about (accuracy, accents/languages, Arabic support, hallucinations, missed handoffs), and what makes deployments succeed?

### Takeaway
The documented complaints are, first, speech recognition failing on regional accents and speech impairments, with no easy route to a human (UK GP case). Second, products overpromised in sales: integrations missing or reduced to basic voicemail. Third, hallucinated policy answers, where the business is legally liable (Air Canada). Fourth, billing surprises (overage, automatic upgrades, separate telephony). Fifth, order calls deflected to text links instead of taken. Deployments that succeed constrain the AI with rules and menu or POS data, guarantee a human fallback, test for dialects (Arabic especially), and measure results against a baseline.

### Cited Findings
- **Accents and speech:** Healthwatch Rotherham said the EMMA AI receptionist (QuantumLoopAI) used by GP practices "does not respond well to variations in speech such as regional accents or those with speech impediments." One patient "could never get it to understand me" and gave up; others went to the surgery in person. Healthwatch wants patients to keep the option to speak to a person; the developer called reception "overwhelmingly positive" — [Management in Practice](https://managementinpractice.com/news/ai-receptionists-can-struggle-to-understand-regional-accents/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/ai-gp-receptionist-stroke-patient-accessible-information-standard); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/ai-receptionist-fails-to-book-patients-appointments-as-it-cant-understand-their-accent-3401507/)
- **Misrecognition stat (unverified):** a vendor blog cites a Consumer Technology Association figure that 68% of regular voice AI users name misrecognition as their top complaint — [NextPhone](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/will-ai-receptionist-make-mistakes)
- **Hallucination and liability:** Air Canada's chatbot gave incorrect bereavement-fare information, and a tribunal held the airline liable (reported via vendor blog) — [NextPhone](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/will-ai-receptionist-make-mistakes)
- **Overpromised integrations and capability:** a Capterra review (Sep 2025, law practice) of My AI Front Desk calls the sales process misleading. - **Support access:** a Trustpilot review of Certus AI complains of being unable to reach anyone during setup. The company replied that it believes the review is fake — [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/certus-ai.com)

## 5. How do these tools connect to CRM, POS and booking systems, and where are the integration gaps an agency could fill?

### Takeaway
Restaurant vendors compete on native POS integrations (Toast, Square, Clover and others) and reservation integrations (OpenTable, SevenRooms, Resy, Tock). Almost none position themselves as a CRM or marketing database, and phone callers are often never captured as contacts. Horizontal receptionists rely on Zapier or webhooks for CRM and field-service software. Those links are one-way, gated behind higher-tier plans (ServiceTitan "The Works," Housecall Pro MAX, the Workiz developer add-on), and sometimes oversold. That leaves the agency four jobs: capture call and chat data into a CRM, build follow-up and reactivation flows on top, connect WhatsApp to booking and POS in the GCC, and report on ROI.

### Inferences
- **Gap 1 — guest/customer data:** restaurant AI phone vendors write into the POS, not into a marketing CRM. An agency can add each caller (phone number, order, visit) to a CRM such as HighLevel, HubSpot or Klaviyo, then run SMS/WhatsApp win-back, review requests and direct-ordering promotions. This turns a cost-saving tool into a revenue tool.
- **Gap 2 — field-service software access:** many home-services SMBs are on ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro or Workiz tiers that block API or webhook access. An agency can audit plan tiers, choose a receptionist with a native connector, or build a middleware sync in Make, n8n or Zapier, and check that two-way status updates work.
- **Gap 3 — missed-call and lead follow-up:** inbound AI answers the call, but follow-up sequences, outbound reactivation (Avoca-style) and speed-to-lead for real estate and clinics sit in the CRM. That is where the agency's outbound and GTM services connect.
- **Gap 4 — GCC WhatsApp to POS/booking:** WhatsApp platforms connect to reservations, but order and POS sync is a premium custom build. A remote agency can fill this mid-market gap with productised Arabic and English WhatsApp ordering and booking bots.
- **Gap 5 — measurement:** vendors report self-selected metrics, so an agency dashboard showing calls answered, orders or bookings captured, revenue per call and handoff rate is a differentiator.
- **Gap 6 — website, ads and SEO:** captured callers and chatters feed retargeting audiences and review generation, which tie into the agency's ads and SEO services.

### Gaps
- No vendor documentation was retrieved on whether Loman, Kea, Slang or Maple expose caller and contact data via API or CRM export. This needs vendor confirmation.
- No data on Toast or Square partner-program restrictions for third-party voice AI.
- No specific integration details for UK/EU booking systems (ResDiary, Dojo, Fresha, Doctolib) or AU/NZ systems (ServiceM8, Tradify, Cliniko). The connectors are probably relevant but were not researched.
